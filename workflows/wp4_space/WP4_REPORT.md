# WP4 — Generality with respect to the spatial discretization

## Objective

WP4 tests whether the edge–Floquet/Nyquist-shedding mechanism found for the cubic-B-spline de Frutos discretization is a peculiarity of that stencil or a broader feature of compact spatial discretizations used for Rosenau–Hyman compactons.

The comparison uses the four legacy schemes studied together in the 2007 article radiation paper:

1. Ismail–Taha, formal orders `(2,2)`;
2. de Frutos, `(6,4)`;
3. Padé-6, `(4,6)`;
4. Padé-8, `(8,2)`.

All calculations use the same absolute `|K|(2,2)` equation, no artificial dissipation, and the same compacton family. The principal cross-stencil spectrum is computed with the two-stage Gauss–Legendre method (GL4), so the conclusion is not tied to implicit midpoint.

## 1. Exact stencil-dependent Nyquist reductions

For a centered five-point method

\[
 \mathcal A_i\dot U+(\mathcal B_i+\mathcal C_i)(|U|U)=0,
\]

Nyquist demodulation `U_j=(-1)^j a_j` is exact. A traveling envelope

\[
 U_j(t)=(-1)^j A F_i\!\left(j-\frac{v}{h}t\right)
\]

satisfies

\[
 V_{i,h}F_i=\mathcal M_{i,h}(F_i^2),\qquad
 v=\frac{A}{h^2}V_{i,h}.
\]

The four exact envelope operators are

\[
\mathcal M_{\rm IT,h}(D)=
 \frac{\sinh D}{D}\,[2(\cosh D+1)-h^2],
\]

\[
\mathcal M_{\rm DF,h}(D)=
10\frac{\sinh D}{D}
\frac{(h^2+12)\cosh D-5h^2+12}
     {\cosh(2D)-26\cosh D+33},
\]

\[
\mathcal M_{\rm P6,h}(D)=
20\frac{\sinh D}{D}
\frac{(h^2+12)\cosh D-5h^2+12}
     {\cosh(2D)-56\cosh D+63},
\]

\[
\mathcal M_{\rm P8,h}(D)=
\frac53\frac{\sinh D}{D}
\frac{42(\cosh D+1)+h^2(5\cosh D-16)}
     {\cosh(2D)-16\cosh D+18}.
\]

Thus the existence of a nonlinear Nyquist envelope and the scaling `v∝A/h²` are structural, while the profile, width, tail and velocity coefficient are stencil dependent.

## 2. Solitary-wave families

At `h=0.02` the computed normalized profiles give:

| Method | `V_h` | FWHM (cells) | profile residual |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Ismail–Taha | 2.85764672 | 3.1860 | 2.7×10⁻13 |
| de Frutos | 20.66052165 | 5.4492 | 2.5×10⁻13 |
| Padé-6 | 40.74963985 | 7.2949 | 2.8×10⁻13 |
| Padé-8 | 31.99019953 | 6.1963 | 3.0×10⁻13 |

The integral identities are

\[
V_{i,h}=\alpha_i(h)\frac{\int F_i^2}{\int F_i},
\]

with

\[
\alpha_{\rm IT}=4-h^2,\quad
\alpha_{\rm DF}=30-5h^2,\quad
\alpha_{\rm P6}=60-10h^2,\quad
\alpha_{\rm P8}=\frac{140}{3}-\frac{55}{9}h^2.
\]

The rational schemes have method-dependent exponential kernel scales

\[
\sigma_{\rm DF}=0.842633,\quad
\sigma_{\rm P6}=0.549422,\quad
\sigma_{\rm P8}=0.708142.
\]

The Ismail operator has no rational denominator poles; its computed envelope is much narrower and its tails decay markedly faster. This observation is numerical/formal, not stated as a tail theorem.

## 3. Exact method-dependent edge seeds

Substituting the sampled compacton-edge expansion into each semidiscrete scheme gives, at leading order,

\[
R_N^{(i)}(\phi)=c^2hD_N^{(i)}(\phi)+O(h^3).
\]

For all four methods the phase dependence is the same antisymmetric factor, but the coefficient changes:

\[
D_N^{\rm IT}=\frac{2\phi-1}{24},\qquad
D_N^{\rm DF}=\frac{2\phi-1}{180},
\]

\[
D_N^{\rm P6}=\frac{2\phi-1}{360},\qquad
D_N^{\rm P8}=\frac{2\phi-1}{280}.
\]

Relative to de Frutos, the leading seed strengths are `7.5`, `1`, `0.5`, and `0.642857`. The universal result is the `O(h²)` perturbation scale after one cell passage; the injection coefficient is method dependent.

## 4. Spatial generality of the edge–Floquet instability

At fixed edge phase `φ_R=1/4`, `h=0.02`, and high-order GL4 with `κ=10`, every spatial discretization is Floquet unstable:

| Method | dominant multiplier | `ρ_F` | branch |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Ismail–Taha | `-1.058004 ± 0.141969 i` | 1.067486 | complex pair |
| de Frutos | `-1.134199` | 1.134199 | real negative |
| Padé-6 | `-1.155974` | 1.155974 | real negative |
| Padé-8 | `-1.132463` | 1.132463 | real negative |

The eigenpair residuals range from `2.0×10⁻12` to `5.7×10⁻11`. The fixed-point residuals are `O(10⁻10)` for de Frutos and Padé-6 and `O(10⁻8)` for Ismail and Padé-8; even in the latter cases the distance `ρ_F-1` exceeds the residual scale by more than six orders of magnitude.

Midpoint calculations at `h=0.02, κ=20` independently give instability for all four schemes, with `1.06596≤ρ_F≤1.15441`. Padé-6 GL4 refinement gives

\[
\rho_F(\kappa=5)=1.1558858,\quad
\rho_F(10)=1.1559743,\quad
\rho_F(20)=1.1559821,
\]

confirming temporal convergence of the strongest alternative-stencil instability.

Therefore, the instability is not generated by the de Frutos mass matrix or derivative stencil. Its multiplier and real/complex character are nevertheless method dependent.

## 5. Natural nonlinear shedding for independent stencils

Natural compacton runs with midpoint, `h=0.02`, `κ=20`, and `φ_R=1/4` were performed for all four stencils.

### Ismail–Taha

The forward field contains a dense train of very narrow coherent packets. Two clean tracks give:

| packet | `A/h²` | profile residual | measured `v` |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| I | 0.65254481 | 1.14×10⁻4 | 1.86346827 |
| II | 0.71253960 | 1.36×10⁻4 | 2.03452804 |

### Padé-8

Two clean packets give:

| packet | `A/h²` | profile residual | measured `v` |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| I | 0.14712620 | 3.48×10⁻4 | 4.70110031 |
| II | 0.12862007 | 2.66×10⁻4 | 4.11090622 |

In both methods, the correlations with the independently computed method-specific profile exceed `0.9999999` over long tracks.

### Padé-6 finite-window result

The Padé-6 numerical compacton is spectrally the most unstable of the four at the principal high-order comparison point. Nevertheless, at the selected phase no clean isolated forward packet with own-profile residual below 2% was found by `T=30`; the best candidate residual was `0.528`. This is not evidence against the linear instability. It shows that the nonlinear route, latency and packet isolation are not determined by `ρ_F` alone. Seed projection, competing modes and nonlinear reorganization remain method dependent.

## 6. Independent free-wave validation

For each Ismail and Padé-8 natural packet, a second simulation was initialized directly with the corresponding method-specific profile at the fitted amplitude. Natural and independently prepared velocities agree to

\[
2.6\times10^{-5},\ 3.1\times10^{-5},\ 8.8\times10^{-5},\ 6.7\times10^{-5}
\]

relative error for Ismail I, Ismail II, Padé-8 I and Padé-8 II, respectively.

This is stronger than merely fitting a shape: independently generated waves of the same discrete family reproduce the natural packet speeds under the same fully discrete dynamics.

## 7. Stencil specificity of the captured profiles

A clean Ismail packet at `t=6` has own-family residual `1.16×10⁻4`. Fitting the de Frutos, Padé-6 or Padé-8 profiles instead gives residuals `0.375`, `0.531`, and `0.448`.

A clean Padé-8 packet at `t=20` has own-family residual `3.48×10⁻4`. The best wrong-family fit is de Frutos with residual `0.0890`; the Ismail and Padé-6 residuals are `0.448` and `0.115`.

Thus the capture is not a generic consequence of fitting any bell-shaped Nyquist envelope. The emitted objects select the nonlinear solitary family of the actual spatial stencil.

## 8. Universal and method-dependent parts of the mechanism

### Universal across the four compact spatial schemes

1. Exact Nyquist demodulation of the centered odd-flux semidiscretization.
2. A nonlinear envelope equation and the homogeneous scaling `v=(A/h²)V_h`.
3. An edge residual with an `O(h)` Nyquist projection and hence an `O(h²)` seed scale.
4. An edge-dominated Floquet instability of the numerical traveling compacton.
5. Nonlinear coherent packets that can be captured by a discrete Nyquist solitary family.

### Method dependent

1. The nonlocal envelope operator `M_{i,h}`.
2. Profile width and tail scale.
3. The coefficient `V_{i,h}` and therefore packet velocity at fixed amplitude.
4. The leading edge-seed coefficient.
5. The unstable multiplier, branch type and growth rate.
6. Shedding latency, multiplicity and whether a clean packet appears in a finite observation window.

## article-level conclusion

WP4 elevates the manuscript from a detailed explanation of one de Frutos artifact to a general computational mechanism:

> A moving low-regularity compacton edge excites a stencil-dependent Nyquist sector. The numerical traveling compacton is edge–Floquet unstable for all four legacy compact spatial discretizations examined. Nonlinear evolution may shed coherent grid-scale packets, and when a clean packet forms it is selected by the method-specific nonlinear Nyquist solitary-wave family rather than by a universal linear group velocity.

The strongest main-text evidence should be: the four profile families, the four-method high-order Floquet comparison, and the Ismail/Padé-8 natural-to-prepared capture validation. Full spacetime diagrams, coefficients, residuals and the Padé-6 finite-window negative result belong in the supplement.
